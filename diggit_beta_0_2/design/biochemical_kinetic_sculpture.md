# DIGGIT biochemical kinetic sculpture — design brief

## Concept
A modular, enclosed, recirculating drip structure that makes carbonate mineral growth visible as a slowly evolving stalactite/stalagmite landscape. A replaceable porous ceramic or mineral scaffold provides nucleation surfaces. A controlled drip manifold distributes mineralizing solution; collection basins return liquid to a sealed reservoir.

## Critical chemistry distinction
- Atmospheric or process-stream CO2 can be captured into an aqueous carbonate/bicarbonate system and, with a calcium source under controlled chemistry, precipitated as calcium carbonate (CaCO3).
- Solid particulate carbon such as soot/black carbon is not transformed into CaCO3 by this process. Capture it separately with characterized filtration media. If artistic inclusion is desired, immobilize a small, characterized fraction in a sealed, replaceable composite element; do not aerosolize or disperse it.
- A carbonic-anhydrase-assisted route is a research option. Ureolytic microbial routes can generate ammonia and create secondary waste; they should not be the default.

## Kinetic and visual system
1. Gas-side CO2 capture module with measured inlet/outlet CO2.
2. Liquid reservoir with pH, temperature, conductivity and alkalinity monitoring.
3. Calcium dosing and controlled carbonate saturation.
4. Low-flow peristaltic pump and drip manifold.
5. Textured, replaceable nucleation scaffold; gravity-driven mineral accretion.
6. Collection sump, recirculation, and a separate maintenance/cleaning loop.
7. Lighting and time-lapse imaging to document growth without implying that visual growth equals verified carbon storage.

## Measurements
CO2 inlet/outlet, pH, alkalinity, dissolved calcium, conductivity, flow rate, temperature, mass of deposited solid, mineral phase (e.g. XRD/Raman), and carbon balance. Record water replacement and cleaning losses.

## Stoichiometric upper bound
For idealized conversion:
CO2 + Ca(OH)2 -> CaCO3 + H2O
1 g CO2 corresponds to approximately 2.274 g CaCO3 and 1.684 g Ca(OH)2. This is a theoretical mass relationship, not an operating recipe or a guarantee of capture efficiency.

## Safety and environmental boundary
Prototype as a contained benchtop installation. Control caustic solutions, aerosols, biological growth and electrical equipment around water. No environmental discharge until effluent is characterized and appropriately treated. Do not describe the installation as net-negative or permanent carbon removal until full life-cycle and carbon-balance evidence supports that claim.

## Research questions
- Which scaffold geometry yields stable, inspectable accretion rather than clogging?
- How do flow, supersaturation, alkalinity and surface texture affect morphology?
- Can a non-ureolytic enzyme-assisted process reduce unwanted byproducts?
- What fraction of input CO2 is retained in verified carbonate after maintenance and cleaning?
